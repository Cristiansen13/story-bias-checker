2 · State of the art

What existing work measures

Bias in NLP

“Bias” is often left undefined; name the harm (Blodgett et al., 2020). Metrics and datasets surveyed by Gallegos et al. (2024). Word embeddings already encode stereotypes (Bolukbasi et al., 2016; Caliskan et al., 2017).

Fixed-format benchmarks

StereoSet and CrowS-Pairs compare sentence likelihoods; WinoBias and Winogender test gendered coreference; BBQ tests answers under ambiguity. LLMs pick the stereotyped occupation 3–6 times more often (Kotek et al., 2023).

Open-ended generation

Regard (Sheng et al., 2019) and BOLD (Dhamala et al., 2021). GPT-3 stories tie women to family and looks (Lucy and Bamman, 2021); reference letters (Wan et al., 2023); marked personas (Cheng et al., 2023).

Measurement tools

LLMs as annotators (Gilardi et al., 2023) and as judges (Zheng et al., 2023), with position and self-preference biases. Active learning asks humans about the most informative examples first (Settles, 2009).

Four groups of work. Blodgett and colleagues showed that “bias” is often left undefined; Gallegos and colleagues survey the metrics. Benchmarks such as StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs, WinoBias and BBQ test whether a model prefers a stereotype it is shown; Kotek found LLMs pick the stereotyped occupation three to six times more often. For open-ended text there are regard and BOLD, and Lucy and Bamman showed GPT-3 stories tie women to family and looks. Finally, LLMs now serve as annotators and judges, and active learning saves human labels.
