1 · Problem

The model fills in what the prompt leaves open

LLMs now write a growing share of stories, teaching material and marketing text. When a prompt leaves a character’s gender, origin, age or job open, the model chooses, and its defaults repeat across millions of texts.

If the surgeon is almost always a man and the night cleaner almost always a minority woman, no single sentence is offensive, yet the pattern is a representational harm.

Research questions

RQ1 How often do readers perceive a stereotype, per model?

RQ2 Which kinds appear, and which premises trigger them?

RQ3 Do model family and model size change the rates?

RQ4 Can an interpretable classifier reproduce the labels and show its evidence?

Scope: English stories of about 250 words · perceived stereotypes, not model intent · text only

Large language models increasingly write fiction, teaching material and marketing copy. Whenever a prompt leaves a character’s gender, origin, age or job unspecified, the model has to choose, and those choices repeat across millions of generations. If the surgeon is nearly always a man and the cleaner nearly always a minority woman, no single sentence is offensive, but the pattern is a representational harm. So we ask four questions: how often readers perceive stereotypes per model, which kinds and which premises trigger them, whether family and size matter, and whether an interpretable classifier can reproduce the labels with evidence.
